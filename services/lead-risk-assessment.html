Lead Risk Assessment for Milwaukee Homes and Rentals

Intact old paint is one thing. Paint that is chipping, dust on the sills and bare soil by the foundation are another. A risk assessment sorts out which you have.

What a risk assessment looks at

A certified risk assessor checks the condition of painted surfaces, collects dust wipe samples from floors and window areas, and may sample bare soil around the building. The report names the hazards found and the controls that would address them.

It is the right first step when dust returns to the same window, when a child has an elevated blood lead result, or when a rental owner needs to know what is actually wrong before deciding on repairs.

Milwaukee's local layer

The City of Milwaukee Health Department runs a childhood lead poisoning prevention program, and its Primary Prevention Program is aimed at making properties lead-paint safe before a child is harmed. If the Health Department has contacted you about a property, bring that paperwork to the first call so the assessment scope matches what was asked.

What comes after

The report may point to cleanup, window work, repainting with proper prep, encapsulation or full abatement. Because each of those is a different job with different rules, a written scope from the assessor makes it easier to compare proposals fairly.

Common questions

How is a risk assessment different from testing the paint?

Testing identifies lead-based paint. A risk assessment identifies hazards, meaning deteriorated paint and friction surfaces plus lead in dust and soil, and tells you what to do about them.

Do I need one if a child lives in the home?

If a child has an elevated blood lead result, or you see chipping paint or window dust in a pre-1978 home, a risk assessment is a sensible step. Your child's doctor and the Milwaukee Health Department can advise on the health side.

Does it fix anything?

No. It is an investigation. The fixing is done by separate lead hazard reduction work, and the report tells the crew what to address.
