Lead-Safe Window and Door Replacement in Milwaukee

Old wood windows open and close against painted surfaces, which is exactly how lead dust gets made. Replacing the worst offenders can take the problem out of daily life.

Why windows come up so often

A double-hung sash rubs against its frame every time it moves. Doors do the same at the jamb and threshold. In a house with lead paint, that friction puts dust on the sill and the floor, right where small children crawl and play.

Renovation or abatement?

The method matters. Replacement done as a paid renovation in a pre-1978 home falls under Wisconsin lead-safe renovation requirements, which include certified renovators and lead-safe work practices. Replacement done as a planned hazard reduction project follows the abatement rules instead. The proposal should say which one you are getting.

Plan around the neighborhood

Milwaukee has many older neighborhoods where original windows are part of the look. If your building is in a historic district, ask the city what review is required before replacing windows, and plan lead-safe work and approvals together.

Common questions

Do I have to replace every window?

Not necessarily. A risk assessment can identify the components that create the most dust and the ones that can be treated another way.

Who should do the work?

In Wisconsin, paid window replacement in a pre-1978 home needs a certified renovator or a certified abatement contractor, depending on the project.

Is cleanup part of the job?

It should be. Ask how the area will be cleaned and verified once the new window is in.
