Lead Paint Encapsulation and Enclosure in Milwaukee

Not every lead surface has to come out. Where the paint is sound and the surface is right, a durable coating or a physical barrier can control the hazard.

Two different approaches

Encapsulation applies a specialized coating that bonds to the paint and seals it in. Enclosure installs a durable barrier, such as new siding, panels or trim covering, so the lead paint is no longer exposed. Both count as lead hazard reduction when done correctly.

When it fits

The surface needs to be in sound condition. Paint that is flaking off in sheets, or a component that rubs and makes dust, such as an old sash, is rarely a good candidate. A risk assessor can say which components could be treated this way and which should be replaced.

Keep it from becoming a repaint

Ordinary paint is not encapsulation. Ask for the product name and the method, and confirm the work is done by certified people in a pre-1978 home. Ask also how the sealed surfaces will be monitored over time.

Common questions

Is encapsulation permanent?

It lasts as long as the coating or barrier stays intact, so the surface needs to be checked over time. Removal and replacement are the options that take the lead paint out.

Can it be used on windows?

Moving parts make dust, so friction surfaces are often better handled by replacement or repair than by coating.

Do I still need testing?

Yes. Testing tells you where lead paint is and which surfaces are candidates for this method.
