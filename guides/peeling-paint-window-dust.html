Peeling Paint or Window Dust? What to Do in an Older Milwaukee Home

You see chipped paint on a porch or a gray film on a windowsill and wonder if it is lead. Here is how to think about it before you scrape, sweep or call anyone.

Do not dry-scrape or sweep it up

Dry scraping, sanding and sweeping spread fine particles. If the house was built before 1978, wet-wipe loose chips with a damp paper towel, keep children and pets away, and hold off on any scraping until you know what is under the paint.

Describe where it shows up

Note the rooms and components: window sills, porch rails, door frames, stair edges. Windows and doors that rub, and exterior paint on porches and trim, are the classic spots. This description is more useful to an inspector than a general sense that the house is old.

Pick the right service

If you want to know whether paint contains lead, ask for testing and inspection. If dust keeps coming back or a child is affected, a risk assessment is the better first step. The Milwaukee Health Department can also answer questions about children's exposure.

Common questions

Is all old paint dangerous?

No. Intact paint is less of a concern than paint that chips, peels or gets ground at windows and doors. That is why condition matters as much as age.

Should I repaint it myself?

Fixing the surface without prep can make dust. In a pre-1978 home, get the paint tested first and follow lead-safe practices.
