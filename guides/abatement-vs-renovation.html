Lead Abatement vs Lead-Safe Renovation in Wisconsin

The same window or wall can be part of two very different jobs. Knowing the difference helps you ask for the right thing and read a proposal.

Abatement permanently addresses a hazard

EPA defines abatement as activity designed to permanently eliminate lead-based paint hazards. In Wisconsin, it must be done by a certified abatement contractor, and in the city of Milwaukee a project permit applies.

Renovation changes the building

EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting program covers paid work that disturbs paint in pre-1978 homes and child-occupied facilities. Wisconsin's lead-safe renovation rule asks contractors to be trained and certified, to give owners and occupants lead-safe information first, and to follow lead-safe work practices. The goal is to do the project without making new lead dust, not to remove the hazard for good.

Put the distinction in the proposal

Ask the professional to name the project type, the certification that applies, how the area will be contained and how it will be cleaned and tested after. If a proposal blurs the two, ask for it to be rewritten.

Common questions

Is a certified renovation firm an abatement contractor?

Not automatically. These are different certifications in Wisconsin.

Which should I ask for?

Start from the purpose. If the aim is to permanently remove a known hazard, ask about abatement. If you are remodeling and want to do it safely, ask about lead-safe renovation.
